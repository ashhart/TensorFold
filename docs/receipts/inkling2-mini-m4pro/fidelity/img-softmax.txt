1. The softmax was at its floor, not slow
   Shape the tower ran: 16 heads x a 256-row chunk x 6,121 keys, an fp32 score matrix of 100.3 MB (one chunk, 24 a layer).
   Standalone (img-sm-bench.m with img-sm-bench.metal: the committed kernels' text, random bf16 q, k, v, GPU time of one dispatch,
   median of 25), quiet machine (load 2.8), three runs, ms: q k^T 1.337 1.337 1.338, softmax 0.883 0.854 0.858, p v 1.765 1.768 1.765.
   The softmax reads and writes the matrix once from DRAM (its three passes over a 24 KB row hit the caches): 200.6 MB at
   250 GB/s is 0.802 ms, so it ran at 1.06 to 1.10 times its floor. 23 layers x 24 chunks x 0.86 ms is 0.47 s of the 0.54 s
   the stage table of img-ttft.txt gave it (that profile ran under load). q k^T and p v are ALU-bound (1.2 and 0.9 TFMA/s over 1.6 GFMA a chunk).

2. What was tried, with the bits of each
   (a) A faster standalone softmax with the same max, exp and sum order: nothing to gain at the floor. One simdgroup a row
       (eight virtual simdgroups of lanes, eight simd_sums, so each of the 256 threads' partial sums and both reduction steps
       are the old ones) is bit-equal and no faster (0.90 ms on bf16). More threads or vector loads either cannot go under the
       floor or regroup the per-thread sum (thread t adds elements t, t + 256, ...), which changes bits; online max-and-sum
       changes the sum order too and was not built.
   (b) Fusing max-subtract, exp and 1/sum into p v's operand fetch, with a row-stats kernel that keeps the old sum order:
       bit-equal (same O hash) but slower: stats 0.22 to 0.23 ms (one read of the bf16 scores) plus p v with the exp in the
       fetch 2.42 ms, against the separate softmax 0.449 plus p v 1.711 ms. The exp costs 0.7 ms inside a compute-bound
       kernel, more than the write and read it saves. Dropped.
   (c) Applied: store the scores as bf16. The softmax's first step rounds q k^T and then q k^T * scale to bf16, and its
       result is rounded to bf16, so every value the matrix holds between the kernels is a bf16 number. q k^T's epilogue now
       stores bf16(bf16(sum) * scale) (as bf16), the softmax reads that and writes bf16(exp(s - max) / sum) in place, and
       p v reads the bf16 probabilities (exact in fp32) as the A operand. Max, the fp32 exps (recomputed in the store pass,
       not kept), the sum's order (a stride of 256 threads, then simd_sum twice) and each p v output's fma chain over k are
       untouched. The matrix is half the bytes, so the scratch holds 512 rows a chunk instead of 256 in the same 256 MiB.
       Per chunk, ms, same runs: q k^T 1.337 -> 1.313, softmax 0.86 -> 0.449 (floor 0.40), p v 1.766 -> 1.711. A layer of the
       invoice (6,121 rows): 24 chunks of 256 rows fp32 = 95 ms; 12 chunks of 512 rows bf16 (2.578 + 0.857 + 2.746) = 74 ms.
